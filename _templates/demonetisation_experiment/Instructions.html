In this task, you will participate in {{ C.NUM_ROUNDS }} rounds. In each round, you receive income in a lab currency called ECU (Experimental Currency Unit). Your objective is to manage your finances across rounds.

At the end of the experiment, your total wealth (Deposit + Cash) will be converted into British pounds (£) at the exchange rate:

1 ECU = £0.01
Structure of Each Round

Each round proceeds as follows:

1. Income

You receive {{ C.ENDOWMENT }} in cash.

2. Deposit Decision

You decide how much of the {{ C.ENDOWMENT }} to deposit into your bank account.

  • The deposited amount is taxed at 30%.
  • Any amount not deposited remains as cash in hand and is not declared for tax purposes.
3. Mandatory Spending

In every round, you must spend exactly {{ C.MANDATORY_SPENDING }}.

You must decide:

  • How much to pay from your deposit account.
  • How much to pay from cash in hand.

The two amounts must add up to exactly to {{ C.MANDATORY_SPENDING }}.

Important: If you choose to pay any positive amount from cash, you will be required to type a randomly generated sequence of letters exactly as shown (case-sensitive). This typing represents the inconvenience of handling physical cash rather than digital payments.

4. Random Audit

After your spending decision, there is a {{ C.BASE_AUDIT_PROB }}% chance of an audit. Only your cash in hand is audited. This includes any cash carried over from earlier rounds and any undeclared cash from the current round.Funds held in your deposit account are safe.

If you are audited:

  • You must pay 30% tax on all cash in hand.
  • You must also pay a fine equal to 2 times the unpaid tax.
  • So the total penalty equals 90% of your cash in hand..
  • The total penalty is deducted from your deposit account first. If the deposit balance is insufficient, the remaining penalty is deducted from your cash in hand.
  • After the penalty is paid, all remaining cash in hand is automatically transferred into your deposit account. This transferred amount is not taxed again, as the penalty has already been served.
5. Round Summary

At the end of the round, you will see your updated:

  • Deposit balance
  • Cash in hand

These balances carry over to the next round.

Example
Example Round

Starting balances: Deposit = 70 ECU, Cash = 30 ECU

You receive 100 ECU in cash.

You deposit 60 ECU:

  • Tax = 30% × 60 = 18 ECU
  • Net amount added to deposit = 60 − 18 = 42 ECU
  • Cash not deposited (from this round's income) = 100 − 60 = 40 ECU

New balances after depositing:

  • Deposit = 70 + 42 = 112 ECU
  • Cash = 30 + 40 = 70 ECU

Mandatory Spending (40 ECU total) — you choose to spend:

  • 20 ECU from cash
  • 20 ECU from deposit

New balances after spending:

  • Deposit = 112 − 20 = 92 ECU
  • Cash = 70 − 20 = 50 ECU

If you are not audited, you end the round with these amounts.

If you are audited:

  • Tax on cash = 30% × 50 = 15 ECU
  • Fine = 2 × 15 = 30 ECU
  • Total penalty = 15 + 30 = 45 ECU
  • Penalty deducted from deposit first: Deposit after penalty = 92 − 45 = 47 ECU
  • Remaining cash (50 ECU) is then automatically transferred into your deposit account
  • Final balances: Deposit = 47 + 50 = 97 ECU, Cash = 0 ECU

Important: Upcoming Policy Change

After Round 7, the current lab currency (old ECU notes, which is held as cash in hand) will become invalid. Old currency (cash in hand) can no longer be used for spending.

The only way to preserve the value of old currency is to deposit it into your bank account. However, depositing old currency comes with two consequences for Rounds 8–10:

  • Higher audit probability: The more old currency you deposit, the higher your personal audit probability during Rounds 8–10.
  • Converted amount at risk: During Rounds 8–10, if you are audited, tax and fines are calculated on both your cash in hand and the amount of old currency you converted. The converted amount is only at risk once — if audited, it is considered fully regularised and cannot be charged again in a later round.

From Round 11 onward, the audit rate returns to 5% for everyone, and only your cash in hand is subject to audit — converted old currency is no longer at risk.
